Series, Sums and Special Values

1. The Basel problem

Euler showed in 1734 that the sum of the reciprocal squares converges to a surprisingly clean value. Writing ζ(s) for the Riemann zeta function, we have

ζ(2) = ∑n=1∞ 1n2 = π26

More generally, ζ(s)=∏p(1−p−s)−1 where the product runs over all primes p and s>1.

2. Partial sums

Let SN=∑k=1Nk denote the N-th triangular number. A short induction shows

∑k=1N k2 = N(N+1)(2N+1) 6

The same technique gives the closed form for the sum of cubes, which is the square of SN:

∑k=1N k3 = (N(N+1)2)2

3. Binomial coefficients

For non-negative integers n≥k the binomial coefficient is (nk)=n!k!(n−k)!, and the binomial theorem states that

(x+y)n = ∑k=0n (nk) xn−k yk

4. A limit

Finally, the exponential function can be recovered from the compound-interest limit

limn→∞ (1+xn)n = ex

which, evaluated at x=1, defines Euler's number e≈2.71828.
