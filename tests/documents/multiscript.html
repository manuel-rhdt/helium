English, with embedded Arabic

Arabic script is cursive: each letter takes an isolated, initial, medial or final form. The word كتاب (kitāb, “book”) is built from the root ك ت ب, which also yields مكتبة (maktaba, “library”) and كاتب (kātib, “writer”). A good typesetter keeps such embedded words intact, in the right order, and on the shared baseline — and handles numerals like 1,234.56 and dates such as 5 November 2026 without fuss. Justification should spread spaces evenly, never letter‑space Latin, and avoid stretching gaps beyond reason.
